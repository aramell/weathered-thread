## 1. NON-NEGOTIABLE BRAND FOUNDATION

- Brand: Weathered Thread
- Master tagline: Made to Remember. Stitched in.
- Brand idea: A sense of place, stitched in.
- Collection language: For places worth remembering.
- Brand philosophy: Made to look better lived in.
- Strategic idea: The embroidery is the medium. The feeling is the product.
- Launch collection: Sea Isle
- Brand architecture: PLACE → STORY → MOTIF → OBJECT

## 3. HOW THE SITE SHOULD FEEL

Understated, coastal, nostalgic, warm, tactile, lived-in, quietly premium, timeless. Avoid souvenir-shop, overly nautical, kitschy beach, generic rustic, corporate, crowded, or trend-heavy styling.

Photography should feel real and tactile: actual garments, actual embroidery, people wearing pieces, natural light, weathered/coastal textures and close stitch detail. Do not use generic stock imagery simply to fill a section.

## 4. SITE STRUCTURE AT LAUNCH

Navigation: SHOP | COLLECTIONS | ABOUT • SEARCH | BAG

Homepage order: Hero → Sea Isle feature → brand idea/story → shop/product grid → embroidery/detail section → email signup → footer.

Keep launch focused on adult apparel. Build the structure so future collections, kids and accessories can be added later without rebuilding the site.

Do not build yet: kids navigation, custom embroidery, personalization, accessories, blog/journal, loyalty, wholesale, complex filtering, discount-heavy promotion, or unnecessary animations/popups.

## 5. PRODUCT PAGES — GARMENT QUALITY IS PART OF THE SELLING STORY

Do not describe these as simply 'embroidered shirts.' The blank garment itself is part of why the customer should buy the piece. Each product page should explain the specific Comfort Colors garment used, its fabric/weight/feel/fit/construction, then explain the embroidery and the place/story behind it.

Recommended product-page sequence: Product images → name/price → color/size → ADD TO BAG → short emotional description → 'THE GARMENT' → 'THE EMBROIDERY' → fit/size → care → shipping/returns.

Use close-up photography to show both garment texture and embroidery quality.

## 7. WHAT IS STILL NEEDED BEFORE THE PRODUCT CATALOG IS FINAL

- Comfort Colors garment-detail sheet/specs for each style (fabric, weight, fit, construction, care, official product language).
- Final product/design assignment: which embroidery goes on which garment.
- Final product names.
- Final sizes offered for each sellable SKU.
- Retail price for each product.
- Final inventory count after any samples/testing.
- Final product photography / lifestyle photography.
- Shipping policy, processing time, returns/exchanges, and final size-chart measurements.

## 8. ANDREW'S BUILD RULES

- Use the exact five brand colors and supporting UI tones above.
- Use Fraunces for expressive serif display type and IBM Plex Mono for supporting mono/details, consistent with the brand system.
- Keep the visual system quiet. Do not add colors, fonts, effects or nautical clichés just because Squarespace offers them.
- Show the garment quality as a selling point, not an afterthought.
- Make Sea Isle feel like a place/story, not a generic beach graphic collection.
- Optimize mobile as carefully as desktop.
- Build flexible collection/product structure, but keep the visible launch experience simple.
- Do not finalize product copy/specifications until the garment-detail information is supplied.

## 9. FINAL BRAND CHECK

If a design decision makes the site feel more generic, more nautical, more promotional, or more complicated than the brand needs, stop and choose the quieter option.
